## 1. nanoGPT: more baselines  (~2–2.5 GPU-h per method, embarrassingly parallel)

The nanoGPT field is 5 methods (Sven, AdamW, Muon, MuonW, SOAP); the perceptron scans have 15.
`exp_nanogpt_speedrun.yaml` runs `optimizers_standard: [AdamW, Muon, MuonW, SOAP]` over
`lrs_standard` = 6 lrs × 5 seeds = 30 runs per method, 50 epochs. Today's grid runs took
2.8 min (AdamW) to 5.0 min (SOAP) each.

Cost estimate per added method = 30 grid runs + 5 confirmation + 1 timing + 1 diagnostics
= 37 runs, scaled by the profiler's nanoGPT step time relative to AdamW (23 ms/step):

| method | ms/step (profiler) | × AdamW | est. per run | est. per method (37 runs) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SGD | 21 | 0.9 | ~2.5 min | ~1.5 GPU-h | **run** |
| RMSprop | 23 | 1.0 | ~3 min | ~1.7 GPU-h | **run** |
| Adam | 23 | 1.0 | ~3 min | ~1.7 GPU-h | **run** (AdamW already there; cheap, completes the pair) |
| SGD + momentum | ~21 | 0.9 | ~2.5 min | ~1.5 GPU-h | **run** |
| Polyak SGD | 29 | 1.25 | ~3.5 min | ~2.2 GPU-h | **run** |
| KFAC | 138 | 5.9 | ~17 min | ~10 GPU-h | maybe — kfac-pytorch profiled fine on nanoGPT, but it fails on both MNIST scans; expect a dash |
| JD (UPGrad) | 265 | 11 | ~30 min | ~19 GPU-h | maybe — feasible, 3 GB, but a day of GPU for one row |

| HIG | — | — | — | — | **infeasible**: output Jacobian is B·L·V = 64·128·65 rows × 826 k params |

**Recommended set: SGD, SGDm, RMSprop, Adam, PolyakSGD** — 5 methods × ~2 GPU-h ≈ 10 GPU-h
total, so on 4–8 GPUs of `lab_gpu_priority` it's a ~2–3 h wall-clock job if launched tonight.
KFAC/JD only if there's idle capacity after that; they don't change the story.

their whole row will be at the top edge.

Three corrections to the spec above, if this is ever picked up:

1. **There is no `lrs_sgd`.** Per-family learning-rate lists exist only for LBFGS
   (`lrs_lbfgs`), JD (`lrs_jd`) and HIG (`lrs_hig`); every "standard" optimizer shares
   `lrs_standard` (`grid.py:137-172`, the loop at `grid.py:548`). MNIST-CE does not use a
   separate key either — it just makes the shared list wide (1e-5 … 1.0, 8 points). So
   step 1 as written cannot be done by config alone: widening nanoGPT's `lrs_standard`
   would add 5 lr points x 4 existing methods x 5 seeds = **100 runs to AdamW/Muon/MuonW/
   SOAP**, which changes their selection pool and the tuning budget the appendix discloses
   (`budget.py` counts distinct trajectories per method) — i.e. it moves numbers the paper
   already reports.
   The clean route is this repo's own precedent, `OFF_GRID_ITEMS` in
   `tests/test_tools_launch.py` (how `p2_cifar_ce_rtol` added the CIFAR-CE rtol points):
   leave `exp_nanogpt_speedrun.yaml` untouched and add plan items that carry their own
   `lrs_standard=` override, so nothing already measured is re-hashed, re-run or re-selected,
   and `campaign/grid_counts.md` / the golden counts / reconcile's expected grid stay put.
2. **PolyakSGD has no learning rate** (`grid.py:638-660`: its own grid over
   `polyak_f_star`/`polyak_max_lr`/`polyak_eps`, all single-valued by default) — it is
   **5 runs, not 30**.
3. **Counts, from the launcher's own composer** (`launch_campaign.check_items`, so these are
   the run_ids that would actually be claimed):
   `optimizers_standard=[Adam,RMSprop]` -> 60; `[SGD,SGDm]` with
   `lrs_standard=[0.003,0.01,0.03,0.1,0.3,1.0]` -> 60; `[PolyakSGD]` -> 5.
   **125 new grid runs, not 150.** At the measured 2.8-5.0 min per run on a full A100 that
   is ~7 GPU-h; on the 8 available MIG slices (~2x slower per run) ~1.5-2 h wall clock,
   inside the 12 h `gpu_test` limit.
4. Also note: with the follow-up studies deliberately left alone, these five methods would
   have **tuning-seed numbers only**. Table 1 and the nanoGPT figure are built from the
   CONFIRMATION seeds (`analysis/lib/headline.py`), so the new baselines could not join
   them without a `*_confirm` pass — they would have to be presented as a separate
   grid-only table, or the confirmation pass has to be run too (5 more runs per method).
